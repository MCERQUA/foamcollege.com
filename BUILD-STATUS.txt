Because the dispatched "certification prep / training curriculum / industry
education" framing would have meaningfully overlapped both sprayfoaminstitute.com
and insulationcollege.com (both are course-catalog / "take our course to get
certified" properties), I pivoted per the dispatcher's own explicit guidance
("if the space is already covered... consider a narrower/different angle... rather
than a duplicate") to a genuinely distinct angle:

FoamCollege = the NEUTRAL NAVIGATOR. It does not sell or deliver a course. It exists
to explain, compare, and demystify the actual certification/licensing landscape:
  - What SPFA PCP actually is (ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation, DoD spec recognition)
  - How manufacturer certifications differ from and relate to SPFA PCP
  - The real, fragmented state-by-state licensing picture (~30 states, no single
    national standard, three different regulatory patterns)
  - How recertification/CEU renewal cycles actually work (and what happens if you
    let a credential lapse)
  - An honest framework for choosing between competing certification programs
    (not a ranked "best one" — matching credential type to your actual goal)
  - What certification does NOT teach you (hands-on technique, business skills) —
    with an honest hand-off to the sibling sites (Institute for courses, Mentor for
    1:1, Bootcamp for business) rather than pretending FoamCollege covers everything

Research grounding: SPFA PCP's ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation and DoD specification
recognition, the ~30-state fragmented insulation-licensing landscape (three regulatory
patterns: dedicated insulation license / folded into general contractor license /
code-compliance-only), and the manufacturer-certification-as-purchasing-gate pattern
(BASF, Icynene-Lapolla/Demilec, Carlisle) are all real, verifiable industry structures
— not invented framing. Where program specifics change over time (exact CEU hour
counts, renewal fee amounts), copy explicitly directs readers to confirm current
requirements directly with the issuing body rather than asserting a specific number
that could go stale.
